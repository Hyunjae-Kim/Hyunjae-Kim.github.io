COMPUTATIONAL AUDITION & MUSIC COGNITION

Understanding hearing.
Connecting music and the brain.

I am a Ph.D. student in the Music and Brain Lab at KAIST, advised by Kyung Myun Lee. I study how we hear and experience music, combining computational models with human brain and behavioral measurements.

My research connects deep audio models with auditory neuroscience: how biological constraints shape learned representations, how these representations align with the brain, and what they can tell us about naturalistic listening.

Before KAIST, I studied physics at Chungnam National University and worked on bio-inspired auditory systems and real-time speech enhancement.

Research interests

Two connected directions
01

Computational models of hearing

I use deep learning and biologically inspired constraints to investigate auditory representations, with a focus on temporal modulation, music selectivity, and alignment between models and the auditory cortex.

02

Music, brain, and listening experience

I combine EEG and behavioral experiments with naturalistic music to study immersive spatial audio, musical emotion, and preference, connecting listening experience to neural responses and audio features.
