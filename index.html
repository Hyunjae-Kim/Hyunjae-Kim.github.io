Auditory perception and music
through the lens of NeuroAI.

I am a Ph.D. student in the Music and Brain Lab at KAIST, advised by Kyung Myun Lee. I study how the brain represents sound and music by combining deep auditory models, EEG, and behavioral experiments. My work focuses on how biological constraints shape auditory representations and how these representations relate to human perception and listening experience.

Research interests

01Computational HearingHow do biological constraints shape auditory representations?

I build deep auditory models to study how cochlear processing and spectrotemporal constraints shape sound recognition and cortical selectivity.

02Music & NeuroAIWhat can music models reveal about human perception?

I compare representations in music foundation models with human brain responses and perception, focusing on auditory cortex alignment and musical emotion.

I use EEG and behavioral experiments with naturalistic music to study immersive spatial audio, music preference, and familiarity.
